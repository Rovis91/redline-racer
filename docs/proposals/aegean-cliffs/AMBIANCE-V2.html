Aegean Cliffs

Mood and reference sheet, V2. Written after the blockout; it follows the five zones of the built map (src/maps/egee/, ?map=egee) and the named blocks already standing there, and keeps the art direction of the V2 bible. Distances are those of the layout at scale 1 (4700 m, 8 to 172 m).

A fictional Cycladic island on a summer afternoon. One closed road: the quay of a working harbour, the old quarry road up a dry flank, a whitewashed village on the crest, a cornice above the open sea, then down through farm terraces to the quay. You start at water level, earn the height, thread the village, breathe on the balcony and fall back to the port: the relief sets the rhythm, and the sea is in the frame almost all lap.

Place
A small steep island: harbour basin on the south side with a mole and a lighthouse, cliffs on the east flank, a plateau with the village at 170 m, capes to the north-west, gentler terraced slopes to the west. Other islands as flat blue silhouettes on the horizon.
Time and light
Early afternoon in July, sun high and slightly behind the left shoulder at the start: hard white light, short crisp shadows, no haze near the car, a pale band of sea haze at the horizon. The meltemi blows: flags, awnings and mill sails all lean the same way.
Style
Retro low-poly: few volumes, clearly ranked, flat faces. Three values carry the picture: dark asphalt, warm stone, white lime. The sea is the saturated surface, lighter and greener where it touches rock. Read at 150 km/h before being read up close.
Life
An island that is lived in and hosts a one-day race: temporary barriers, bunting and a few stands, always off the roadway. Buildings are braking markers, never an excuse for a side road.
Anti-references
Santorini postcard overload (a blue dome on every roof); a resort with pools, hotels and yachts; big-city or futuristic buildings; lush or tropical green; grey northern rock; sunset orange; ruins of temples and columns; any house, stair or junction on the drivable band.

ZONE 1 · 0 to 650 m · 8 to 20 mPort: the quay

Start on the 16 m quay road, a long left-hand sweep round the basin with the town front on the inside and the water on the outside, then the first rise as the buildings stop and the cliff takes over.

Mood
A fishing harbour on race day: boats still tied up, nets drying, the whole island stacked above the start line.
Landmark
The white lighthouse at the end of the mole, across the basin (blocks PHARE and DIGUE); behind the grid, the village as a white line on the crest, which is where the lap is going.
Read at speed
  • a continuous white town front two to three storeys high, blue and green shutters, striped awnings at ground level (FRONT DE MER)
  • sand-coloured warehouses with wide doors, crates and nets stacked against them (ENTREPOT)
  • three wooden pontoons and small painted boats, coral, blue and white hulls (PONTON, BATEAU)
  • a pale stone quay edge with bollards, turquoise water right below it
  • the rock wall closing the end of the quay (FALAISE): the signal that the climb starts
Light
Full sun on the facades, the brightest and most colourful zone. Awning shadows fall on the pavement, not on the road. Water glitter on the outside of the bend.
Transition
The built front stops on one clean edge; walls turn from lime to rock, colour drops out, the road tilts up.
Not this
A marina of white yachts; a container port with cranes; a ferry blocking the view of the basin; cafe tables on the roadway.

ZONE 2 · 650 to 2000 m · 20 to 161 mAscension: the quarry road

1350 m of climb at up to 12.6 %, linked medium bends on a dry flank (tightest radius about 59 m), cliff on the inside, a drop and the bay on the outside, the first two houses of the village at the top.

Mood
Conquering the height: rock, heat and wind, the harbour shrinking in the mirrors at every bend.
Landmark
The cut cliff face standing over the road (FALAISE), and above it the white rim of the village, seen again and again from below and closer each time.
Read at speed
  • warm fractured rock in big faceted planes, ochre in the sun and red-brown in shadow, horizontal strata
  • pale dressed-stone retaining walls on the inside of the bends (MUR SOUTENEMENT), their top line drawing the curve ahead
  • a low white-capped parapet on the sea side wherever there is a real drop
  • olive terraces below the road: grey-green tufts in rows on red earth (OLIVERAIE)
  • the bay as a flat cyan plane filling the outside of every left-hander
Light
Raw sun on ochre; the rock faces alternate lit and shaded planes, which is what makes the bends readable. No tree shade. The sea is at its most saturated seen from above.
Transition
The slope eases, rock gives way to a first low white wall, then two houses either side (MAISON at 1940 and 1975 m): the field of view narrows into the village.
Not this
Alpine hairpins with steel guardrails; pine forest; a tunnel; scree or boulders on the roadway; black volcanic rock.

ZONE 3 · 2000 to 2800 m · 161 to 171 mVillage: the perched chora

The signature passage, almost flat on the plateau: the road narrows to 12 m between houses, a counter-curve of 38 m radius near 2150 m, the square and the chapel at 2400 m, then the houses thin out toward the edge.

Mood
Precision in a corridor of white walls: dazzle, then a slice of cool shade, then dazzle again.
Landmark
The blue dome of the chapel above the roofs (CHAPELLE, COUPOLE), set back from the road beside the square (PLACE). It is the only blue dome of the island.
Read at speed
  • white cubic houses with flat roofs and rounded corners, staggered in height, standing straight on the road edge (MAISON)
  • blue doors and shutters, one colour per house, on otherwise blank walls
  • magenta bougainvillea as a few big masses on walls and pergolas, placed at corner exits
  • outside stairs, porches and low parapets, all on the house side of the kerb
  • the open paved square: the one wide gap, pale sand floor, where the sky comes back
Light
The highest contrast of the lap: lime walls close to white-out, dark asphalt, hard shadow bands across the road where houses stand close. Shade is blue-grey, never black. Apexes and exits stay in the sun.
Transition
The last pair of houses opens like a gate: the sea is suddenly back on the right and the road takes the edge of the plateau.
Not this
Lanes too narrow to race; pastel Italian facades or tiled pitched roofs; a wall hiding an apex or an exit; crowds, tables or flower pots in the run-off; neon and shop signs.

ZONE 4 · 2800 to 3500 m · 171 to 123 mBalcon: the cornice

Wide balcony curves on the rim of the plateau (12 to 14 m wide, radius 64 m at the tightest), high rocks on the inside, the void and the capes on the outside, then the road tips into the western descent.

Mood
The breath of the lap: wind, emptiness, the horizon at eye level and the sea very far below.
Landmark
The windmill on the high point at 2900 m, 40 m back from the road (MOULIN): white round tower, thatched cap, bare sail spars against the sky.
Read at speed
  • a continuous low stone parapet with a white cap on the sea side, the only line between road and void
  • tall warm rock on the inside (HAUTES ROCHES), broken into a few big blocks
  • capes stepping out into the sea one behind the other, each paler than the last (CAP)
  • isolated cypresses as dark vertical strokes on the sky (CYPRES)
  • two bands of sea: deep blue offshore, cyan against the foot of the cliffs
Light
The most open sky. Light comes from the sea side; the parapet cap and the mill are the white accents, everything else is rock and blue. The village stays visible behind as a white silhouette.
Transition
Past the last cape the parapet gives way to field walls, the rock softens into steps, and the road drops into the terraces.
Not this
A row of five mills (one is enough); a viewing platform with railings and coaches; a rock on the racing line; fog hiding the drop.

ZONE 5 · 3500 to 4700 m · 123 to 8 mDescente: the terraces

1200 m down the western slope at up to 14 %, successive counter-curves (radius 79 m at the tightest) between stepped fields, olive groves on the inside, a ruin far off the road, then the first harbour houses and the line.

Mood
Falling in rhythm through farmland, left, right, left, with the harbour basin growing in the windscreen.
Landmark
The basin, the mole and the lighthouse of zone 1, seen from above and reappearing at each right-hander: the target of the whole descent.
Read at speed
  • long dry-stone walls stepping down the slope like contour lines, pale stone over red-brown earth (TERRASSES)
  • olive groves as rows of round grey-green crowns with dappled shade under them (OLIVERAIE)
  • pairs of cypresses marking the changes of direction (CYPRES)
  • a roofless stone farm on a far terrace (RUINE), same stone as the walls
  • the first lime walls, lamps and painted signs of the port on the last 300 m (MAISON DU PORT)
Light
Sun on the driver's side slope: warm stone, the only soft shade of the lap under the olives, on the verge and not on the road. Harbour colours come back gradually: first white, then coral and blue.
Transition
The slope flattens, the walls close into a street, the quay edge and the water return on the outside: the lap ends where it started.
Not this
Green irrigated fields or lawns; Tuscan avenues of cypress; vineyards on wires up to the kerb; a farm track joining the road.
