Area 51 / Signal Unknown

Mood and reference sheet, V2. Written after the blockout was validated; it follows the zones of the built map (src/maps/area51/), not the layout of the V5 bible, and keeps the bible's art direction.

A fictional flight-test base in a Nevada basin at blue hour. The race runs on the base's own service roads: the hangar line, a reactor complex under the mesa, a listening dish in its bowl, the security ridge, then the runway. Speed under surveillance: everything is utilitarian and still switched on, and something beyond the range is transmitting.

Place
Dry lake bed to the north, twin runways on its edge, the base on the toe of an alluvial fan, mountains closing the basin south and west.
Time and light
Blue hour, sun just down behind the western range: violet sky, a band of orange on the horizon, long soft shadows. Artificial light is sparse and warm outside (amber beacons, sodium floodlights), cold inside.
Style
Retro low-poly: faceted volumes, flat readable surfaces, simple contrasts, segmented asphalt. Read at 150 km/h before being read up close.
Mystery
Never shown. A glow above the western crest, a hum, dishes that all point the same way. No saucer, no creature.
Anti-references
Chrome science-fiction base; explicit UFO; a civilian airport (jet bridges, terminals, logos); a modern glossy military showroom; ruins. The base is in use, a little worn, 1960s to 1980s concrete and steel.

ZONE 1Hangars: the flight line

Start on the apron in front of the hangar doors, a chicane through the fire alley, then H1, a maintenance hall crossed from gable to gable between four prototype airframes on their docks.

Mood
Workshop at shift change: huge doors left open, an aircraft half dismantled under the trusses, nobody in sight.
Landmark
The mouth of H1, 40 m wide, seen from the start line; inside, the white airframes alternating left and right.
Read at speed
  • olive corrugated steel walls, a sand-coloured concrete plinth
  • roof trusses as a regular rhythm overhead, strip lights between them
  • airframes as pale wedges on amber scaffolding docks
  • yellow tow line painted on a dark polished floor
  • red crash tender and foam island in the alley
Light
Outside: violet sky on dull steel, amber apron masts. Inside: even cold white, darker than outside but never black; both doors visible at once.
Transition
Out of the west door the base thins out: back shops, water tower, then bare fan and the berms of the guard post.
Not this
An airliner hangar with airline colours; a tidy museum; crates on the lane.

ZONE 2B1: the complex under the mesa

A guard post and its concrete serpentine, a ramp that dives 18 m under the mesa, a security lock, the gallery round the research reactor above its pool, the labs cavern with its internal tram, the silo, and out by the same flank.

Mood
The Black Mesa facility of Half-Life, as a place and not as a copy: a government lab buried in desert rock, built for an accident nobody admits could happen. (Named as a mood only; no image of the game is used.)
Landmark
The blue glow of the reactor pool, seen from the gallery through the bend.
Read at speed
  • raw board-marked concrete, rounded tunnel sections, thick door frames
  • yellow-and-black hazard bands, trefoil signs, monitoring arches at the lock
  • blue pool light on the walls, the polar crane in amber above it
  • armoured doors and observation windows of the test chambers
  • the tram rail overhead between lock and labs, pipe runs on the walls
Light
Regular cold ceiling lights, no black holes; warm rock outside the portals; one colour event, the blue of the pool. The exit frames violet sky and the first dishes.
Transition
The west portal opens on the bowl: rock gives way to gravel and white steel.
Not this
A cave with stalactites; a clean white science-fiction corridor; green slime and glowing barrels; water on the road.

ZONE 3Antenna field: the listening post

Down into the bowl and half-way round D1, the 120 m dish, then north along the array arm where smaller dishes stand on pads beside a rail line.

Mood
Wind, a low hum, a machine the size of a stadium turning very slowly toward something.
Landmark
D1, seen from the west portal of B1, then circled. It is the landmark of the whole lap.
Read at speed
  • off-white dish, grey lattice back structure, a massive pale concrete pedestal
  • the dish above the rim of its bowl, the road looping round its foot
  • low olive shelters, fenced compounds, closed gates
  • twin rails and the transporter track along the arm
  • the smaller dishes alternating sides, all pointing the same way
Light
The most open sky of the lap. White dishes catch the last orange on one side and go violet on the other; small red obstruction lights.
Transition
The dishes shrink in the mirrors; the road turns west and starts to climb, the ground gets rockier.
Not this
A satellite-TV dish farm; radomes everywhere; a pylon on the racing line.

ZONE 4Ridge and perimeter: the security side

Up the ridge to the hairpin round the telemetry pad, down through a road cut, between the munitions igloos behind the spur, along the fence and its watchtowers to the main gate.

Mood
The breath of the lap: more sky, more rock, fewer buildings. You are being watched from far away.
Landmark
The telemetry dish and its mast on the crest; then the gate canopy ahead, with the hangars behind it announcing the line.
Read at speed
  • faceted red-ochre rock, mesas with a hard cap, dry washes
  • earth-covered igloos: grass-less mounds with a concrete front and a steel door, in rows
  • chain-link fence with barbed wire, warning signs, lattice watchtowers
  • the gate: two small booths, a striped barrier, no narrowing
  • the glow of the signal above the western range
Light
Against the light on the climb, the range in silhouette; the playa pale and flat to the north on the way down.
Transition
Tarmac thickens, beacons return, the road crosses the runway end between barrier arms.
Not this
A fortress wall; a border checkpoint with lanes; green hills; an object flying in the glow.

ZONE 5Runways: the sprint

From the gate: the controlled crossing of the runway end, the parallel taxiway, a connector, then runway A at full width with no guardrail, the angled exit and the tight right back onto the apron.

Mood
Controlled emptiness. The widest, fastest, flattest place of the lap, with a plane lined up on the twin runway.
Landmark
The control tower on the hangar line, and the aircraft on runway B.
Read at speed
  • dark tarmac with white runway markings: threshold bars, numbers, centreline, touchdown blocks
  • yellow taxiway lines and hold bars, black-and-yellow taxiway signs
  • amber edge lights flush with the ground, approach light bars west of the threshold
  • the pale lake bed to the north, with no horizon but the far range
  • tower, fire station, light masts and fuel tanks along the apron
Light
Very long vanishing lines; cold violet far away, points of amber on the ground, the tower cab lit from inside.
Transition
The exit brings the hangar doors back into view: the lap closes on the start line.
Not this
A racetrack painted on a runway (kerbs, tyre walls, advertising); a civilian terminal; a plane crossing the race.
