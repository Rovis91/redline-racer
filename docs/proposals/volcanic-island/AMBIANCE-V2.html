Volcanic Island / La Couronne Brisée

Mood and reference sheet, V2. Written after the blockout was validated; it follows the zones of the built map (src/maps/volcan/, 6.58 km, run anticlockwise with the sea on the right), not the layout of the bible, and keeps the bible's art direction.

A fictional Atlantic island born of its volcano, in the family of Madeira's cliff roads and the red-and-black ground of the Canaries and Cape Verde. The race runs on the old service road of the volcano observers: out of the fishing harbour, up the terraced ash slope, round the black needle at the pass, through the crater itself, and back down to the quay by the hairpins. You do not drive round a volcano: you cross every layer of an island, from its daily life to its core of rock.

Place
One island, sea all round. A closed summit crater (rim near 360 m) in the middle, a basalt needle at the north-east pass, sea cliffs and stacks on the roadless west and north coast, the only harbour on the south shore.
Time and light
Clear afternoon, sun in the south-west, sky slightly veiled toward the horizon. Hard readable shadows outside; the crater floor in shade, lit by its own lake; the galleries dark with sparse warm lamps.
Style
Retro arcade drift, PS1 / Saturn: faceted low-poly, 64 to 128 px textures in nearest filtering, flat readable surfaces. Ocean and basalt dominate; vegetation is occasional. Read at 150 km/h before being read up close.
The volcano
Quiet, watched, not erupting. Liquid lava exists in one place only, the lake on the crater floor (a decision of the author after the blockout; the bible had none). Everywhere else: cold flows, scoria, steam.
Anti-references
Liquid lava everywhere, red sky or ash plume; dense jungle; road ribbons floating without structure, or junctions that look like the circuit; a resort island (hotels, marina, golf); a big town at the harbour.

ZONE 1Le Phare: the working end of the harbour

PK 0 to 690, 12 to 40 m. The start leaves the quay eastwards: the last house fronts and the café, three low sheds, the crane and its crates on the quay edge, boats at anchor, then black rock as the road begins to climb.

Mood
A small fishing harbour on a working afternoon: nets drying, a crane left mid-swing, the sea flat and very blue beside you.
Landmark
The harbour crane, its jib over the quay edge, on the right as the cars leave the line.
Read at speed
  • low sheds with a single pitched roof, cream and pale concrete, doors open on the dark
  • bright hulls (coral, white, blue) and thin masts against dark water
  • stacked wooden crates and coiled rope on a wide basalt quay
  • red beacons and a low cream wall drawing the sea-side edge
  • a few palms, spaced, never a row of trees
Light
Clear afternoon, sun high behind the left shoulder, sky slightly veiled toward the horizon. Warm fronts, cold water, hard short shadows under the sheds.
Transition
The sheds stop, the quay narrows to a ledge, black rocks stand on the island side: the ash slope of Les Cendres opens ahead with the volcano above it.
Not this
A marina with white yachts and glass; a container port; a town avenue. Three to five buildings, set back from the racing axis.

ZONE 2Les Cendres: the terraced ash slope

PK 690 to 2210, 40 to 177 m, climbed. A long run of S-bends up old consolidated ash: three steps of black stone terraces with vines, two hamlets with a chapel and a cistern, plots, scree at the foot of the bank, the sea always on the right.

Mood
Dry, dusty and lived-in: people farm what the volcano left, one low wall at a time.
Landmark
The volcano itself, seen whole for the first time on the left, its crater rim cut against the sky; nearer, the white chapel of the first hamlet.
Read at speed
  • stepped black dry-stone walls running with the contour, green strips between them
  • grey-brown ash ground, rust where it is scoria
  • hamlets of four to six small cubes, cream and rust, flat or single-pitch roofs
  • a white chapel with a square tower and a round cistern beside each hamlet
  • low sparse bushes on the sea side, never above the wall
Light
Open and bright; the slope faces east and south, so the walls catch the light and throw thin stepped shadows. The sea glitters on the right.
Transition
The terraces thin out above 170 m, the ground turns red-brown, the needle appears ahead: the pass.
Not this
A lush green valley; a farm track that looks like a branch of the circuit; houses on the apex of a bend.

ZONE 3La Couronne: the horseshoe round the needle

PK 2210 to 3780, 177 to 232 to 200 m. The signature corner: a wide horseshoe round a 105 m basalt needle and its scree, the pass at 232 m with the belvedere deck and the observation post on the sea side, slag fields and small scoria cones, then the road turns its back on the sea and heads for the volcano.

Mood
Monumental and windy: one black spike, a huge cone behind it, and half the ocean under the guardrail.
Landmark
The black needle inside the bend, with the volcano directly behind it on the approach.
Read at speed
  • a single dark spire, faceted, isolated, never on the roadway
  • red-brown scoria fields and small cones against charcoal ground
  • the belvedere: a pale concrete deck, a railing, three benches, an antenna on the post
  • the outer flank of the volcano rising steeply 120 m from the inside of the bend
  • sea stacks far below, off the roadless north coast
Light
The highest and most exposed light of the lap: pale sky, hard contrast between charcoal rock and the bright concrete of the post. A faint steam on the flank, nothing glowing.
Transition
Leaving the horseshoe the road drops into a cutting: rock walls close in on both sides and the mouth of the north gallery appears.
Not this
A needle that hides the next corner; lava on the surface here; a visitor centre with a car park.

ZONE 4Le Cratère: through the volcano

PK 3780 to 4890, 200 to 112 m. A cutting, the north gallery (200 m), then 320 m in the open across the crater floor: the lava lake lies level in its pit on the left, 3 to 14 m below the road, cooled black crust on the right, a spatter cone, bombs, three fumaroles, the observers’ hut. The closed rim stands 200 m above, with a bench halfway up the wall. Out by the south gallery (260 m).

Mood
The one place on the island where the mountain is still awake: dark, enclosed, hot on one side, and you are driving through it.
Landmark
The orange disc of the lava lake, seen the moment the north gallery ends.
Read at speed
  • the ring of cliffs all round, a horizontal bench halfway up
  • one orange surface with black rafts of crust and two brighter spots
  • matt black crust and scattered boulders on the other side of the road
  • thin pale columns of steam, a red-brown spatter cone beyond the lake
  • two gallery mouths cut in the rock, plain, with a warm lamp line inside
Light
In shadow for most of the floor: the rim hides the sun, the sky is a bright disc overhead. The lake is the only warm light and tints the near wall and the left side of the car. The galleries are dark with sparse warm lamps; the south exit frames the sea.
Transition
The south gallery ends on the open south-west flank: sudden sea, sun and the harbour 100 m below.
Not this
Lava anywhere else on the map; rivers of lava across the road; red sky, smoke plume or eruption; a clean concrete motorway tunnel.

ZONE 5Les Lacets: the hairpins above the harbour

PK 4890 to 6100, 112 to 12 m. Three hairpins down the south-west flank: hillside houses and their outbuildings between the legs, three banana groves, black terraces and gardens on the outer side, the white chapel and its bell tower outside the second hairpin, a pair of palms at the first and the last.

Mood
Sunny and domestic after the crater: gardens, washing-line colours, the harbour getting bigger at every turn.
Landmark
The white chapel and bell tower outside the second hairpin: the braking mark of the descent.
Read at speed
  • houses as small bright cubes (cream, ochre, coral) stepping down the slope
  • banana groves as low, dense, bright-green blocks: the only saturated green of the map
  • black stone terraces with a green strip on top
  • white chapel, square tower, alone on its turn
  • the whole harbour and the lighthouse visible below from each leg
Light
Full afternoon sun on a south-west slope: the warmest and most colourful light of the lap. Long view to the sea haze.
Transition
The last hairpin lands on the quay: the road flattens, widens, and the house fronts line the left.
Not this
A dense jungle of bananas; a suburb of villas; a second village centre competing with the harbour.

ZONE 6Les Quais: the home straight

PK 6100 to the line, 12 m. The quay straight: two rows of houses on the island side, the fish market and the boat workshop on the quay, crates, nets drying, mooring posts, boats and a pontoon in the basin, the breakwater and the lighthouse at its end.

Mood
Arrival: the centre of the island, warm fronts, the lighthouse dead ahead to the right.
Landmark
The lighthouse at the end of the breakwater, white with a red band, visible down the whole straight.
Read at speed
  • a front row of two-storey houses in ochre, cream and coral, tiled roofs, a second row behind
  • the long low red-tiled fish market on the sea side
  • nets laid flat, crates, a line of mooring posts on the quay edge
  • small open boats in the basin, the dark line of the breakwater
  • the start gantry and flags
Light
Low afternoon sun from the right over the water, warm on the fronts, the lighthouse lit against a darker sea.
Transition
Past the line the fronts give way to the sheds and the crane of Le Phare: the lap closes.
Not this
A second harbour; a street narrowed by buildings on both sides; a tall resort hotel.

GAPSReferences not found, and what was not checked

  • Breakwater lighthouse, white with a red band. No free image found of a small harbour light of this kind on a breakwater in Madeira or the Canaries; the two lighthouses above give proportions only.
  • Madeiran terraces (poios) in black stone with vines, seen side-on from a road. The searches returned general views; the Fogo enclosures stand in for the stone and the ash.
  • Inside of an old unlined road gallery (rough rock vault, sparse lamps). Only a short tunnel mouth was found.
  • Harbour house fronts facing a quay, and stacked hairpins with retaining walls. The images found show boats and a valley view; neither shows the thing itself.
  • Small harbour crane and a fish market building of this region: none found.
  • A basalt needle shaped like ours (a pointed spire): Roque Cinchado gives the framing with the cone, not the shape.
