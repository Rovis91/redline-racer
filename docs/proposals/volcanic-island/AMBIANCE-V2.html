Volcanic Island / La Couronne Brisée

Mood and reference sheet, V2, fourth edition. Rewritten after the author's fourth round of driving; the art direction is taken up again completely. The layout is the bible's road, unchanged (6.58 km, anticlockwise, sea on the right, two galleries through the caldera wall): only the scenery and the zones change. The chainages and corners quoted in the zones below were written for a draft layout that the author refused; the chainages in force are in the header of each src/maps/volcan/scenery-volcan-<zone>.js, and the village lies along the harbour straight, not on streets the road drives through. The terrain is now an island designed on its own: a hand-drawn shoreline with bays, headlands and lava deltas, sea cliffs as high as the land behind them, a highland of stepped basalt flows rising northwards, lava lobes ending in scarps, a cone cut by ravines, and a caldera rim broken into crags and saddles with one collapsed sector. The road is laid on it with cuts and embankments.

A fictional subarctic volcanic island (Iceland, Faroe, Lofoten, Svalbard) with two worlds. Below, a fishing village that lives from cod and sheep, its wooden houses in every bright colour. Above, the volcano as the ground of a scientific expedition. Between them: the service road the race runs on, the power line, the hot-water pipeline, the supply track and the signs. Every object has a reason to be exactly where it is; nothing is set beside the road to fill a gap. A pasture belongs to a farm (house, barn, track, gate); an instrument belongs to a survey line that leads to the base camp; a rack of fish stands where the boats land.

Place
One island about 2 km across. Village and harbour on the low south shore; farmland on the east side; a highland ending in 140 m sea cliffs in the north; the caldera in the middle (rim about 300 m, floor at 140 m, radius 200 m), its rim broken, the road crossing its wall in two galleries; a parasitic cone on the roadless west flank; black skerries and stacks off the coasts.
Time and light
Late summer afternoon at high latitude: low clear sun from the south-west, long shadows, a pale cold sky, haze on the sea horizon.
Style
Retro arcade drift, PS1 / Saturn: faceted low-poly, 64 to 128 px textures in nearest filtering, flat readable surfaces. Read at 150 km/h before being read up close.
The volcano
Quiet, watched, not erupting. Liquid lava in one place only, the small lake on the caldera floor; steam vents elsewhere.
Anti-references
Objects dropped beside the road with no owner (a sheep pen with no farm, a lone hut, a row of identical cairns every 150 m); smooth rounded hills and a round coastline; pale blue patches on the volcano (the glacier tongues and blue-shadowed snow of the third edition are removed: snow is white, only on the highest crags); palms, terraces, stucco (first V2); a container port or marina; pastel houses.

ZONE 1Le Port: the quay and the fish racks

PK 0 to 490, 3 to 8 m. The start: the long straight along the quay, heading east, the harbour basin on the right. This is the only flat, sheltered ground on the island, so everything that arrives by sea lands here, the expedition's supplies included.

Mood
A working harbour in cold air: tar, salt fish, wet paint, diesel. Busy but small.
Landmark
The red and white lighthouse at the end of the breakwater, and the tall A-frame cod racks. Both are visible from the grid.
Read at speed
  • quay of basalt blocks, 3 m above the water, dark grey with a pale concrete coping: it gives the right-hand edge of the straight a hard line
  • trawlers (15 to 20 m) and open boats moored at the quay and in the basin, hulls in bright bands, because the village lives from cod
  • a concrete slipway with a boat hauled up on it and a winch, where boats are repaired
  • boathouses (naust) gable to the water, one colour each, white corner boards: boats are kept dry here in winter
  • the fish market shed, long and low, on the quay: the catch is sold where it lands
  • A-frame racks of drying cod, 5 to 6 m high, pale grey wood, on the quay where the catch is landed and on the windy point by the breakwater: wind dries the fish
  • net sheds with nets hung to dry, stacks of fish crates
  • breakwater ending in a red and white lighthouse, which marks the harbour mouth
  • on the left the first rows of the village climbing the slope
  • an expedition depot at the west end of the quay: orange containers, fuel drums, a crane truck. The supply track starts here, so the depot has to be here
Light
Low sun from the south-west across the basin: the water is a dark steel blue with glitter, the painted fronts on the left are lit full on.
Transition
The quay ends and the road turns left into the main street.
Not this
A container port or marina; a crane over the road; racks on the road edge with no boats near them; pastel colours.

ZONE 2Le Village: the street to the church

PK 490 to 900, 8 to 20 m. Two right-angle corners: left into the main street, right round the church square. This is the first braking of the lap. The village grew along the street that climbs from the harbour to the church, so the houses follow the street and the street follows the slope.

Mood
Closed in, bright and domestic after the open quay: paint, fences, washing, a school flag.
Landmark
The white church with its spire on the square at the second corner. It is the braking mark.
Read at speed
  • wooden houses close on both sides of the street and in three or four rows behind it, about 80 houses in all, every neighbour a different saturated colour (green, yellow, blue, red, pink), white trims, dark roofs, some gable to the street
  • small fenced gardens and washing lines: people live here
  • a shop with a sign and a school with a flagpole, a little larger than the houses, so the street has a centre
  • the church: white timber, a pointed dark spire, on the square at the second corner, with a churchyard bounded by a low stone wall and white crosses
  • street lamps along the main street, because the winters are dark
  • the village is far bigger than in the third edition: it is the one place where colour is dense
Light
Sun rakes down the street from the right; shadows of the houses fall across the road and give a rhythm of light and shade.
Transition
The last houses, then the first pasture walls.
Not this
Pastel or stucco houses; tiled roofs; two identical neighbours; a church with no square or street to explain it; palms.

ZONE 3Les Fermes: farms on the old flows

PK 900 to 1900, 20 to 85 m. A fast left sweep out of the village, a 260 m straight through farmland, then two hairpins up the scarp of an old lava flow. The only soil on the island lies on the old flows of the east side, so this is where the village keeps its sheep and cuts hay.

Mood
Orderly and rural: walls, tracks, sheep, wrapped bales. The last place where people own the land.
Landmark
The red barns, and behind the hairpins the black scarp of the lava flow.
Read at speed
  • four farms, each with a farmhouse in one bright colour, a red barn, a turf-roofed sheep shed, a gravel track and a gate from the road: no pasture without a farm
  • pastures enclosed by dry-stone walls that run in straight lines up to the scarp, so the walls show the slope
  • sheep inside the walls only, small white and grey dots on moss-green grass
  • hay bales wrapped in white plastic in the mown fields: the farms cut hay for the winter
  • a round stone sorting fold (réttir) at the foot of the scarp where the farm tracks meet: the autumn round-up ends here
  • the power line on wooden poles and the hot-water pipeline on concrete saddles leave the village along the road, so the road already serves the expedition
  • a road sign before the hairpins, one warning only
  • the lava scarp: black columns, 15 to 20 m high, behind the hairpins
Light
Open and bright. The scarp is in shadow when the sun is behind it, which makes it the darkest shape on the horizon.
Transition
The walls stop at the top of the scarp; moss and bare lava begin; the first orange survey stakes.
Not this
A sheep pen with no farm; a lone hut; fields in strips or terraces; sheep on the road side of the wall; trees.

ZONE 4Les Falaises: the expedition highland

PK 1900 to 3480, 85 to 155 m. The climb across the highland to the cliff-top hairpin at the north tip, then west past the basalt needle to the base camp at the mouth of the north gallery. The expedition's ground starts where farming stops, so every object here belongs to a survey line that leads to the camp.

Mood
Exposed and technical: wind, moss, cliffs, orange flags, instruments.
Landmark
The basalt needle (a volcanic plug, 100 m) that the road wraps, then the orange of the base camp.
Read at speed
  • stepped basalt flows covered in grey-green moss, black boulders never in rows: the highland is built of flows that each end in a step
  • survey lines of orange stakes with flags crossing the road at right angles and running to instruments: the lines are what the expedition measures along
  • a seismic station (a white box, a solar panel, a short aerial) at the end of each line
  • a weather station on the open plateau: a mast, an anemometer, a white louvred screen, placed where nothing shelters it from the wind
  • the cliff-top hairpin at the north tip: a radio relay mast and a guard rail, sea stacks and skerries 140 m below
  • the supply track of the tracked vehicles branching off the road: two ruts with orange stakes
  • the base camp on a levelled gravel pad: rows of orange and white containers, yellow and orange domed tents, an antenna mast with guy wires, a field of solar panels, fuel drums, two tracked vehicles parked, a flag, crates
  • the power line ends at the camp's transformer; the hot-water pipeline goes into the gallery
Light
The highest and most open light. The orange and white of the camp are the only warm notes on grey-green and black.
Transition
The basalt headwall and the gallery mouth.
Not this
A row of identical cairns every 150 m; instruments with no line leading to them; snow or pale blue patches on the moss; a lone hut.

ZONE 5Le Cratère: the drilling rig and the lava lake

PK 3480 to 4270, 155 to 140 to 110 m. The north gallery, the caldera floor crossed southwards, the south-west gallery. This is what the expedition came for. The rim is broken into crags and saddles with one collapsed sector, and the road passes through the intact wall in two galleries.

Mood
Inside the mountain under an open sky: black walls, steam, one orange eye of lava, and cables.
Landmark
The 25 m derrick of the drilling rig, and the white observatory dome on the rim.
Read at speed
  • inner walls as cliff over scree fans, dark grey with a few white crags at the top
  • the lava lake east of the road, the only liquid lava on the island, cordoned by stakes, with gas sensors on tripods around it
  • the drilling rig beside the road: a 25 m lattice derrick, mud tank, generator container, pipe racks. The road bends because the rig is there
  • cable runs on low trestles from the rig and the sensors to a junction box, then up the inner wall on small pylons to the observatory hut, a white dome on a platform on the rim
  • steam vents on scoria mounds, each with a sensor
  • a tracked vehicle and a container shelter at the rig
Light
The floor is in the shadow of the west rim; the east wall is in full sun. The lake and the orange stakes are the only warm light.
Transition
The south-west gallery.
Not this
Lava anywhere else; a red sky or an ash plume; blue ice in the crater; sensors with no cable; an open cutting instead of the galleries.

ZONE 6La Descente: the old summer road

PK 4270 to 5780, 110 to 9 m. Out of the gallery, a traverse of the cone's flank through a rock cut, then three hairpins down to the west shore. This is the old road to the village's summer pastures, reused by the expedition.

Mood
Long and open, bare at the top and greener at every turn, with the sea growing below.
Landmark
The parasitic cone in the west with a survey mast on top, and the view over the sea.
Read at speed
  • ravines and spurs of the cone, scree fans, bare black lava high up
  • a seismic station at each hairpin on its survey line
  • the cable from the caldera coming down on poles to a relay hut, so the instruments above have a link to the camp
  • lower, moss then grass: two summer farms (shieling), each with a turf house, a stone fold and its track
  • sheep on the unfenced hill in loose flocks near the folds, because the hill is open summer grazing
  • the hot-water pipeline following the road down from the gallery
Light
The south-west slope in full low sun: the warmest light of the lap, on cold colours.
Transition
The shore.
Not this
Walls or fields on the open hill; a fold with no turf house; instruments with no survey line; snow below the rim.

ZONE 7La Côte: the shore road home

PK 5780 to 6230, 9 to 4 m. The shore road back to the harbour, flat out. It is the old coast track to the west landing, still used by the boats that land there.

Mood
Flat, fast, bleak and salty: the lap closing.
Landmark
The village sign, then the expedition depot and the head of the quay.
Read at speed
  • a black sand and shingle beach, with skerries offshore
  • boats hauled up on the beach by a boathouse: the west landing is a beach landing
  • racks of fish on the shingle bank, where the wind is steady and the boats land
  • a farm on the landward side with walled hay fields, the same economy as the east farms
  • the village sign and a speed sign: the road is about to enter the village
  • the expedition depot and the head of the quay, where the supplies come in
Light
Low sun over the sea on the right, then the lit fronts of the harbour ahead.
Transition
The quay, the start line.
Not this
A resort beach; a car park; pastel houses; a lone hut.

LINKSLinks between the two worlds

  • The road. An old pasture track, widened for the expedition. It is the reason the race exists.
  • The power line. Wooden poles from the village up the east side to the base camp, ending at its transformer.
  • The hot-water pipeline. On concrete saddles from the caldera down the south-west side to the village: the village is heated by the volcano.
  • The supply track. Two ruts with orange stakes made by the tracked vehicles, beside the road across the highland, with a branch that cuts the loop of the tip.
  • Signs. The village name, a sheep warning, "expedition ground", the gallery height.
