# Moodboard — provenance

Tool used: built-in Imagegen, `stylized-concept` mode. Image saved as `moodboard-zones.png`, then inserted unedited into a documentation board with the computed plan. These are ambience concepts, not game captures or geometric plans.

## Exact prompt

Use case: stylized-concept. Create a cohesive environment moodboard for SUNKEN HIGHWAY, a PS1/Sega Saturn retro 3D arcade racer in a modern city abandoned for decades, partly submerged in clear turquoise water, lush greenery, calm golden morning sunlight. One image, portrait format, FIVE equal wide horizontal panels stacked top to bottom, thin cream gutters, absolutely no text. Each panel is a different game camera scene: 1 Elevated ring expressway 24 metres above water, broad asphalt straight with faded white lane markings, concrete barriers, planted abandoned skyscrapers framing open sky. 2 Smooth sweeping interchange connector on intact concrete columns, one roadway visibly crossing above another with substantial clearance, not a tangle, readable open curve. 3 Grand continuous intact curving viaduct 32 metres high with a breathtaking view of turquoise flooded city and a distinctive twin tower with green terraces; huge peaceful open sky. 4 Dry intact low causeway FOUR metres above the turquoise water, white concrete parapets, submerged ground floors of modern buildings, distant upper deck overhead, no water on track. 5 Wide sweeping uphill expressway climbing through trees growing OUTSIDE barriers, clear asphalt and apex visibility, vines hanging down from outside parapets. Visual language authentic 1996 racing-game rendered screenshots: extremely low polygon counts, angular cuboid buildings, flat shaded polygon tree crowns and billboard foliage, visibly pixelated low-resolution textures, nearest-neighbor texture sampling, limited palette, simple water plane with tiny pixel glints, no realistic reflections, no smooth raytracing, no bloom, no modern concept-art painting. Broad 18 metre wide roads built with plausible continuous geometry. No cars needed, no people, no HUD, no logos. Not gloomy, not disaster rubble, no broken bridges, no photorealism. The panel scenery is illustrative art direction, not a map.
