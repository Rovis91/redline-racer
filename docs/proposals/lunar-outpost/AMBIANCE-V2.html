Lunar Outpost

Mood and reference sheet, V2. Written after the blockout; it follows the zones of the built map (src/maps/lune/, layout V3: about 5 300 m, clockwise), not the five sectors of the bible, and keeps the bible's art direction.

A small 1970s lunar colony between impact craters, as if the space programmes of that decade had gone on past their brochures. The race leaves the base through its airlock, winds round a first crater, runs up a ridge and throws the car off the rim of an immense one. The void for scenery: a few human objects, very small, in front of very large holes.

Place
A fictional Moon. Five impacts: the western crater (Ø 545 m), the great eastern crater (Ø 720 m, floor 100 m below its rim), the south basin (Ø 444 m), a satellite impact, two fresh ones. The colony sits on the plain between the western crater and the south basin.
Time and light
Lunar morning. One hard white sun, low enough to fill the crater walls with shadow; a black sky with stars; no haze, no fog, no gradient at the horizon: far relief is as sharp as near relief. The Earth is small, blue, and stays in the same place in the sky.
Style
Faceted low-poly with a pixel grain: simple geometric volumes, flat surfaces, hard contrast. Three to five things to read per camera frame at speed, one major landmark per zone.
Human scale
Domes, short cylinders, lattice masts, orange guidance marks, ivory rails. Analogue technology: dials, hatches, painted numbers. Everything human is a minority in the frame.
Gravity
The map plays with it once, at the jump. It is not announced anywhere else; the cars drive normally.
Anti-references
A megalopolis or an industrial city; a giant secret base; permanent neon and violet glow; chrome science fiction; open-cockpit rovers with a visible driver; vegetation of any kind; atmosphere (dust clouds, haze, blue sky); detailed stones on the driving surface; a suspended road without a visible restraint; an endless tunnel.

ZONE 1The base and the start airlock

PK 5080 to 500, through the line. The start is in the middle of a 300 m straight, inside a 160 m hall with a door frame at each end; out of it, the habitat dome fills the outside of the first bend, with modules, control tower, hangar and solar fields around the road.

Mood
A pressurised garage opening on nothing: bright and close inside, then the door frame, the dome, and black sky.
Landmark
The ivory habitat dome (70 m), dead ahead when leaving the hall. Second: the exit door of the airlock itself, seen from the grid.
Read at speed
  • inside the hall: repeated ivory frames, an orange band at wheel-arch height, blue-white ceiling panels, the open door as a black rectangle with the dome in it
  • the dome: triangulated ivory panels, darker joints, one row of small lit windows
  • short cylinders lying along the road, each with a painted number and a hatch
  • a lattice control tower and a tall orange antenna, the only verticals
  • matte blue solar fields as flat dark slabs, set back from the road
Light
The only warm and enclosed light of the lap is inside the hall (ivory and amber, cold blue from above). Outside: sun on ivory, almost white; the shadow side of every module is nearly black. Orange beacons on the rails, lit but not blinking.
Transition
The beacons space out, the last tanks pass on the right, rock takes over and the road starts to wind.
Not this
A city; a glass greenhouse full of plants; a science-fiction door with animated light strips; crates and vehicles on the roadway.

ZONE 2Western crater: the chicane and the relay hairpin

PK 500 to 1340, climbing from 22 to 64 m. The road swerves between two fresh impacts that cut the old track, follows the rim of the western crater, and goes round the telemetry relay in a hairpin.

Mood
First contact with the mineral: the base is behind, the road is alone on the edge of a hole, and something has hit the ground here recently.
Landmark
The telemetry dish on the outside of the hairpin. Before it, the two fresh impacts, paler than everything around them.
Read at speed
  • fresh impacts: a dark bowl, a bright ray of ejecta across the grey ground, a few thrown blocks on the outside of each swerve
  • the western crater on the right: a grey funnel with two or three terraces and a floor in full shadow
  • the ivory rail, continuous on the crater side
  • the relay: a small ivory cabin, an orange dish on a short mast
  • faceted boulders in bands, well back from the road
Light
Monochrome. The sun rakes the rim: the outer slope is pale, the inside of the crater is a mass of cold shadow. Copper-orange only at the guidance points.
Transition
After the hairpin the crater leaves the frame, the ground hardens into a ridge and the road straightens.
Not this
A building on the crater panorama; rubble on the road; smoke or glow in the fresh impacts.

ZONE 3The ridge: round the crater, hairpin, run-up to the rim

PK 1340 to 3000, 63 to 70 m. A winding ridge that goes round the great crater by the north, six bends between rocky flanks, a tight right-hand hairpin at its top round the link antenna, then a 220 m run-up with nothing beside it: the road rises 3 m at its end and stops on the rim. The run-up, the centre of the crater and the tunnel are on one line.

Mood
A runway that ends in the sky. The last 150 m are empty on purpose.
Landmark
The orange link antenna on the outside of the hairpin; then the Earth, small and blue, straight ahead above the end of the road.
Read at speed
  • two rocky flanks of different heights, alternating sides, 30 m back
  • a paler ground on the ridge than on the plain
  • the rails stop before the lip; the road edge is the last line
  • far dark ridges on the northern horizon
  • the crest of the crater appears as a pale line only at the last moment
Light
Sun behind and to the left: the road is fully lit, the flanks throw long shadows across it at the start, none at the end. The Earth is the only colour.
Transition
The ground drops away under the car and the whole crater opens at once.
Not this
A ramp with stripes, arrows or a jump sign; a bend before the lip; a huge Earth filling the sky.

ZONE 4The great eastern crater: the jump, the arena, the way out

PK 3000 to 3920. The car leaves the rim at 73 m and lands on the floor at -30 m, about 6 s later, near the middle. The crater is a bowl: a flat floor 400 m across, pitted with some thirty small craters, and a wall that curves up ever steeper to a sharp crest; it is driven anywhere, up to 50 m above the floor. The only way out is a tunnel that leaves from the edge of the floor, straight ahead of the jump, and goes into the wall as a covered way; no light or beacon points to it.

Mood
Six seconds of silence above a bowl the size of a town, then a playground with no exit in sight.
Landmark
The crater itself, seen whole from the air: its wall in rings, the opposite rim 720 m away with the small science station on it. It is the landmark of the whole lap.
Read at speed
  • from the air: four dark strata on the wall, a pale crest all round, the floor as a lighter disc
  • small craters of very different sizes, each with a dark bottom and a pale raised rim
  • no road, no rail, no beacon on the floor: bare regolith, the car's own tracks
  • the station on the far rim, above the tunnel, tiny: an ivory box and a dome, for scale
  • the way out: a plain ivory frame at the edge of the floor, dead ahead after landing, and the covered way behind it going into the wall
Light
The strongest contrast of the lap. The western half of the wall, under the lip, is in shadow; the eastern half and the floor are in full sun. Slate and blue in the shadows. The tunnel mouth is on the shadow side.
Transition
Through the frame: black for an instant, then the cold light of the tunnel.
Not this
Arrows, blinking lights or a lit portal at the exit; a road painted across the floor; a stadium (stands, barriers, banners); a central peak or anything that blocks the landing.

ZONE 5South basin: the tunnel, the ramp, the way back

PK 3920 to 5130. A 280 m tunnel under the rampart that the two craters share, out on the floor of the south basin (-28 m), a ramp that climbs its wall at about 8 % to the rim (20 m), then the maintenance area, three open airlock frames, and the colony again.

Mood
Infrastructure after the void: the lap becomes a service road again, and climbs back to where people are.
Landmark
The buggy garage on the rim, then the three orange frames in a row, with the start hall behind them.
Read at speed
  • in the tunnel: ivory segmented frames, an orange band, blue ceiling panels; short, the exit visible from the entrance
  • the ramp cut into the basin wall: rock steps above it on the left, the basin floor falling away on the right behind the ivory rail
  • the garage: a plain ivory box with one large door; a parked closed buggy, a blue tank
  • three thin orange frames across the road, evenly spaced
  • modules and a warehouse closing in on both sides before the hall
Light
Cold and even in the tunnel, with amber at wheel height. The ramp climbs out of the basin's shadow into the sun about half-way. Beacons return with the garage.
Transition
The last frame gives the door of the start hall: the first image of the lap comes back.
Not this
A long or branching tunnel; pipes and racks on the roadway; a spiral motorway; an industrial zone round the garage.
