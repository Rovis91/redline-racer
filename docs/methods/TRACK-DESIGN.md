# Redline Racer: track design guide

> **Goal:** turn a scenery idea into a **production-ready track concept**: layout, relief, zones, mood, moodboard and first assets.
>
> **Scope:** framed brainstorming and creative pre-production only. No optimisation, code, detailed technical tests or exhaustive asset production.
>
> **Guiding principle:** *a track that is a pleasure to drive, set in a believable world, with an identity you recognise at once.*

## 1. Method in six decisions

Go **in order**: a step that is not validated stays open. Do not compensate for a weak layout with more scenery.

| Step | Question to answer | Minimum deliverable | Validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Promise** | Why does this map deserve to exist? | Pitch + driving experience + signature | Different from existing tracks |
| **2. Territory** | Where are we, and why is there a road? | Concept map, relief, landmarks | Coherent geography |
| **3. Layout** | What do we do at the wheel? | Top view + elevation profile + corner sequences | Rhythm, anticipation, racing lines |
| **4. Zones** | Which stages do we pass through? | 4-6 named sectors, each with a function | Clear, memorable progression |
| **5. Moodboard** | What visual and audio feeling? | Annotated references + palette + lighting | Consistent, readable identity |
| **6. Assets & art direction** | Which objects are enough to make the map recognisable? | 5-10 key assets + composition rules | Ready for a first blockout |

**Stop point:** do not start detailed modelling until a **concept sheet**, the **layout**, the **zone breakdown** and the **moodboard** are validated.

## 2. Design rules to keep in view

### 2.1 The track first

1. **One dominant driving intent** per map: speed, drift, precision, relief, overtaking or visual exploration. The other feelings stay secondary.
2. **Flowing curves before obstacles.** Every corner has a readable entry, a controllable line and a usable exit. Avoid gratuitous piles of 90° corners.
3. **A rhythm**, not a string of difficulties: acceleration, anticipation, manoeuvre, relaunch, breather.
4. **A signposted challenge.** The road, barriers, topography or visual landmarks must help the player understand the next corner. A blind section must be learnable, not arbitrary.
5. **Room to play.** Drift corners need width and length; race sections must allow overtaking. Very tight passages stay exceptional.
6. **Relief has a function.** A climb prepares, a crest reveals, a descent accelerates, a hairpin organises a change of level. Avoid slope breaks that are purely decorative.
7. **Mastery is rewarded.** The player must be able to improve their lines and nitro use, not gain time by exploiting a wall or an unintended shortcut.
8. **A spectacular difficulty can stay accessible.** A cliff, bridge or huge factory can create visual tension without artificially narrowing the whole road.

**Starting proportions, not mandatory:** mostly flowing curves, a few technical sectors, one or two spectacular moments. The right mix depends on the car and the track.

### 2.2 A believable world

9. **The road must have a reason to exist.** It links a harbour to a village, serves a quarry, skirts a cliff, serves a base, and so on. Scale can be exaggerated without losing that logic.
10. **One dominant landmark, 2 to 4 secondary ones.** The player must be able to describe the track as something other than "lots of corners".
11. **The landscape reads at three distances.** Near: speed and kerbs. Middle: buildings, trees, machines and landmarks. Far: the silhouette of the world, mountains, sea, volcano, skyline.
12. **The scenery tells an activity.** Objects are placed according to their function and history: traffic, work, housing, abandonment, water, wear, vegetation.
13. **Light and colour serve readability.** The road stands out from the terrain; a tunnel entrance or an important corner stays visible. Contrast takes priority over detail.
14. **Reveal rather than show everything.** A climb, tunnel or building can hide the panorama before a spectacular opening. Reserve the big reveals for one or two places.
15. **A living but non-distracting world.** Wind, birds, steam, smoke, lights, water, sounds and machines reinforce the mood without hiding the driving.

### 2.3 The filter against endless brainstorming

For every proposal, answer in this order:

- **Does it add a new driving experience?** If not, rework or drop it.
- **Does it strengthen the identity of the place?** If not, do not add it.
- **Is it perceptible at racing speed?** If not, simplify it or reserve it for a landmark.
- **Does it serve a specific zone?** If not, put it in reserve, not on the immediate programme.

**Do not develop** secondary assets, micro-decoration, complex systems or cosmetic variations before the layout and the zones.

## 3. Detailed design process

### Step 1: state the track's promise

Write **5 sentences at most**:

- **Place and inspiration:** real, fictional or hybrid; state the liberties taken.
- **Dominant feeling:** what the player does at the wheel.
- **Emotion:** wonder, speed, vertigo, tension, calm, mystery...
- **Signature image:** the scene the player will remember after a lap.
- **Difference:** what the map brings compared with existing tracks.

**Validation:** the concept stays identifiable without a long list of objects.

### Step 2: draw the territory before the road

On a very simple diagram, place:

1. **Terrain masses:** sea, mountains, crater, quarry, valley, town, plateau, void.
2. **Altitude levels:** high / medium / low, with the major transitions.
3. **Main landmark** and secondary reference points.
4. **Functional places:** harbour, village, factory, base, observatory, tunnel entrance, and so on.
5. **Natural constraints:** cliffs, water, slopes, walls, buildings, impassable areas.

Then decide **why the road crosses these places**. The track need not copy a real geography faithfully; it must feel plausible in its own universe.

### Step 3: draw the layout and its elevation

Produce **two drawings**, without exception:

- **Top view:** complete loop, race direction, key corners, straights, connections, bridges and tunnels.
- **Longitudinal profile:** start, climbs, crests, descents, flat parts and stacked levels.

For each major corner, annotate its **type** (fast, tight, hairpin, sequence), its **intent** (drift, braking, acceleration, precision) and its **visibility**.

Questions to settle:

- Are there enough stretches where the player can accelerate and relaunch the nitro?
- Are the difficult corners readable early enough?
- Do the drift sectors have the length and space they need?
- Do stacked roads, bridges and tunnels make sense in the terrain?
- Do the most spectacular places stay pleasant to drive?
- Can we identify the zones where several cars can overtake each other?

**Validation:** the track is already interesting even if every piece of scenery is replaced by simple volumes.

### Step 4: split the lap into 4 to 6 zones

Each zone needs **a gameplay function and a scenery function**.

| Field | To define |
|---|---|
| Name | An easy-to-remember name: "Harbour", "Ridge", "Furnaces"... |
| Position | Place in the lap and transition to the next zone |
| Terrain | Relief, width, surface, bridge, tunnel, walls... |
| Driving | Speed, drift, braking, overtaking, relaunch... |
| World | Architecture, vegetation, machines, rock formations... |
| Landmark | What lets you recognise the sector at once |
| Staging | Light, reveal, sound, movement, view of what comes next |

**Typical organisation, to adapt:** introduction to the place, rising rhythm, technical passage, contrast / breather, signature moment, finale / return.

**Validation:** each zone differs from the previous one; transitions make geographic sense.

### Step 5: build a useful moodboard

Do not just gather "pretty pictures". Collect references **sorted by what they solve**:

| Category | Look for | Decision to extract |
|---|---|---|
| Geography | Relief, natural formations, scale | Overall silhouette and altitudes |
| Road | Surface, perceived width, barriers, curves | Road language and integration |
| Architecture | Typical buildings, infrastructure | Dominant shapes and materials |
| Vegetation / ground | Rock, water, trees, sand, dust | Spread of biomes and textures |
| Light / weather | Time of day, sky colour, haze, contrast | One main lighting mood |
| Details of life | Activities, objects, movement, wear | A few believable narrative elements |

**Recommended format:** 8 to 12 annotated images, a palette of 5 to 7 colours, 5 mood keywords, 3 visual prohibitions.

Each image carries a note: **"I keep: ... / I do not borrow: ..."**. A reference is not an obligation to reproduce the whole scene.

Per-element reference sheets (two sources per element, 3 to 5 features recognisable at 150 km/h) live inside the map's proposal folder, `docs/proposals/<map>/`.

### Step 6: define the first assets and the art direction

Create a shortlist of assets **by role**:

- **1 hero asset**: volcano, excavator, antenna, rock pillar, and so on.
- **2 to 3 major structures**: village, blast furnaces, hangars, viaducts, domes...
- **2 to 3 modular families**: buildings, rocks, barriers, vegetation or technical modules.
- **1 to 3 mood accents**: flags, steam, birds, beacons, dust, waterfalls...

For each, specify: **silhouette**, **scale relative to the car**, **zone of appearance**, **visual function**, **importance** (essential / secondary).

The mini art bible must fix:

- **Shapes:** round, angular, vertical, massive, repetitive, organic...
- **Materials:** 3-5 dominant ones; new, weathered, rusty, damp, dusty...
- **Palette:** dominant, secondary, accent and road colour.
- **Light:** time of day, direction, temperature, shadows, haze, contrast.
- **Density:** busy zones, open zones, deliberately empty spaces.
- **Animation / sound:** a few characteristic movements and sounds.
- **Prohibitions:** elements that would betray the mood or hurt readability.

**Final validation:** an artist or level designer can build a first blockout without inventing the structural choices.

## 5. Library of seven validated concepts

These directions are **starting points**, not final layouts. The quarry and the steelworks form a single map; the radio telescopes are part of Area 51. Working files for each map live in `docs/proposals/<map>/`.

### 01. Aegean Cliffs · Cyclades, Greece

- **Pitch:** climb from a harbour to a white village clinging to the cliffs, then a panoramic descent over the Aegean Sea.
- **Driving:** flowing curves, big elevation changes, a few wide hairpins; avoid the right-angled urban maze.
- **Possible zones:** harbour, coastal climb, white village, balcony road, windmill / ridge, descent.
- **Moodboard:** Santorini, white houses, blue domes, bougainvillea, turquoise sea, dazzling sun.
- **Palette:** off-white, deep blue, cyan, sand stone, floral pink.
- **Key assets:** windmill, blue domes, modular houses, terraces, stairs, low walls, boats.
- **Signature:** panoramic corner above the harbour, the whole village visible on the slope.
- **Avoid:** lanes that are too narrow and repeated sequences of 90° corners.

### 02. Volcanic Island · fictional island inspired by Madeira

- **Pitch:** leave a colourful harbour, climb the flanks of an island volcano and descend back to the Atlantic.
- **Driving:** coastal roads, basalt tunnels, corniches, climbs and long descents.
- **Possible zones:** harbour, hillside suburbs, cliff / tunnel, volcano shoulder, panorama, ocean descent.
- **Moodboard:** Funchal and Madeira, black cliffs, coloured villages, secondary subtropical vegetation, discreet fumaroles.
- **Palette:** anthracite basalt, deep green, ocean blue, terracotta, cream light.
- **Key assets:** volcanic cone, modular cliffs, tile-roofed houses, tunnels, waterfalls, steam, banana trees.
- **Signature:** big curve facing the volcanic cone with the ocean below.
- **Avoid:** repeating the existing tropical mountain map; volcanic rock and sea dominate.

### 03. Iron Pit · quarry and steelworks

- **Pitch:** drift down to the bottom of a terraced quarry, then cross a monumental metallurgical complex.
- **Driving:** chained wide hairpins, long slides, level changes, industrial relaunches.
- **Possible zones:** ridge, upper terraces, descent spiral, quarry floor, conveyors / blast furnaces, climb back.
- **Moodboard:** open-pit quarry, dust, rust, steel, giant machines, low raking light.
- **Palette:** beige limestone, steel grey, rust, charcoal, glowing orange.
- **Key assets:** giant excavator, mining terraces, conveyors, blast furnaces, cranes, wagons, silos.
- **Signature:** seeing the future road levels under the car and an enormous machine at the bottom.
- **Avoid:** corners so short they break drifts; a factory too dense on the racing line.

### 04. Sky Pillars · Zhangjiajie, Hunan (China)

- **Pitch:** drive between vertiginous rock pillars rising from a sea of clouds, with bridges and corniches.
- **Driving:** curves along cliff faces, high bridges, natural tunnels, precision without blind traps.
- **Possible zones:** low forest, ascent, first pillar, suspension bridge, summit / pavilion, misty descent.
- **Moodboard:** Zhangjiajie, vertical sandstone, pines, discreet Chinese pavilions, waterfalls, mist and dawn light.
- **Palette:** sage green, forest green, sandstone grey, bluish mist, warm white.
- **Key assets:** modular rock pillars, bridges, pavilions, clinging pines, waterfalls, low clouds.
- **Signature:** a bridge over the void, surrounded by hundreds of rock columns.
- **Avoid:** fanciful floating mountains; visibility hidden by fog all the time.

### 05. Area 51 / Signal Unknown · secret Nevada base

- **Pitch:** high-speed race through a fictional military site, its radio telescopes and hangars; a discreet anomaly in the sky.
- **Driving:** long straights, fast curves, a wide test track, entry into the complex.
- **Possible zones:** approach desert, antenna array, checkpoint, hangars, test track, hills.
- **Moodboard:** Cold War, Nevada, giant radars, bunkers, industrial lighting, purple sky at blue hour.
- **Palette:** sand, midnight blue, slate purple, concrete, signal red.
- **Key assets:** large radio dishes, radars, hangars, control tower, bunkers, fences, red beacons.
- **Signature:** an immense antenna dominating the layout; a brief glow behind the mountains.
- **Avoid:** an explicit alien invasion; piling up sci-fi gadgets with no military logic.

### 06. Lunar Outpost · retro-futurist moon base

- **Pitch:** race around a small human settlement, on ridges and along the edge of an immense lunar crater.
- **Driving:** large open curves, mineral relief, a pressurised tunnel, a feeling of isolation; usual car physics.
- **Possible zones:** base, grey plain, ridges, crater rim, basin, tunnel / return.
- **Moodboard:** 1970-1980 space science fiction, industrial modules, lunar grey, space black, hard shadows.
- **Palette:** cold grey, off-white, deep black, very discreet technical blue.
- **Key assets:** large crater, cylindrical modules, domes, antennas, solar panels, rover, pressurised tunnel.
- **Signature:** Earth visible above the crater, a tiny base far below.
- **Avoid:** terrain crammed with buildings; gravity effects that change the driving without need.

### 07. Sunken Highway · drowned motorway

- **Pitch:** cross an abandoned city on viaducts, flooded and reclaimed by lush nature under morning sun.
- **Driving:** large motorway curves, interchanges and slip roads, gradual height variation.
- **Possible zones:** high ring road, overgrown viaducts, submerged district, slip road at water level, giant interchange, return to height.
- **Moodboard:** turquoise water, planted skyscrapers, vines, trees, golden reflections, light haze.
- **Palette:** lush greens, water blue, weathered concrete grey, morning gold.
- **Key assets:** giant interchange, viaducts, modular planted facades, rusty signs, trees, vines, water surfaces.
- **Signature:** a long aerial curve with the whole city partly drowned below.
- **Avoid:** a dark, destructive post-apocalyptic mood; vegetation that hides the road.

## 6. Definition of "ready for a first blockout"

A map is defined enough when **all** of the following exist:

- [ ] Two-sentence pitch, dominant driving and dominant emotion.
- [ ] Explicit difference from the other tracks.
- [ ] Territory diagram and justification of the route.
- [ ] Top-view layout with the direction of travel.
- [ ] Elevation profile, bridges, tunnels and level transitions.
- [ ] Split into 4 to 6 zones with gameplay, scenery and transition.
- [ ] Dominant landmark, secondary landmarks and signature sequence.
- [ ] Moodboard of 8 to 12 **annotated** references.
- [ ] Palette, light, weather, materials, density and sound principles.
- [ ] Prioritised list of 5 to 10 assets, with silhouettes and scales.
- [ ] Three artistic / design prohibitions.
- [ ] No more than three open arbitrations.

**Final deliverable per map:** a mini-dossier made of a **concept sheet**, **two diagrams (plan + relief)**, a **zone map**, an **annotated moodboard** and a **list of hero/modular assets**. Everything else comes after. Once ready, the map moves on to `NEW-MAP.md`.

## 7. Reading list

These sources inspired the rules; they are neither a direct consultation of experts nor normative obligations for an arcade game.

- Luke McMillan, *A Rational Approach to Racing Game Track Design*, [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/a-rational-approach-to-racing-game-track-design): racing lines, width, relief and readability.
- Benoît Gomes, *Racing Level Design: The Rally Case*, [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/racing-level-design-the-rally-case): places, plausible roads, landmarks and progression.
- David Shaver & Robert Yang, *Invisible Intuition: Blockmesh and Lighting Tips to Guide Players and Set the Mood*, [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025360/Level-): lighting and guidance through the environment.
- FIA, [Circuit Safety](https://www.fia.com/circuit-safety): real motorsport reference, to adapt freely to arcade feel.
- Simon Tomlinson & Nic Melder, *Representing and Driving a Race Track for AI Controlled Vehicles*, [Game AI Pro](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter39_Representing_and_Driving_a_Race_Track_for_AI_Controlled_Vehicles.pdf): racing lines and track representation (optional reading at this stage).
